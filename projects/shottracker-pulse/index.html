ShotTracker Pulse

A more accessible game-day product in the ShotTracker ecosystem, designed to help new programs get value quickly with bench-ready video and stats, while creating a natural path toward the fuller Helix experience.

Role
Lead designer
Focus
Product strategy • UI/UX • Rebrand
Platform
iOS iPad
Company
ShotTracker/DD Sports

Design Challenge

01

Accessible entry point

Pulse needed to feel valuable on its own for schools with smaller budgets, while still introducing them to the broader ShotTracker ecosystem.

02

Lower-friction workflow

Coaches needed faster access to video, stats, and game context without the installation effort or workflow depth of a full sensor-based analytics suite.

03

Brand cohesion was fragmented

Parts of the product ecosystem still carried DD Sports naming and store imagery, which created confusion for customers who knew the company and product family as ShotTracker.

Key Product Decisions

Decision 01

Clarify Pulse’s role in a unified ShotTracker ecosystem

Pulse was a lower-cost, lower-setup access point into ShotTracker. The product experience needed to communicate immediate game-day value, align with the broader ShotTracker app family, and avoid feeling like watered-down Helix functionality.

Decision 02

Refresh Helix UX/UI while preserving the upgrade path

I proposed a Helix refresh that improved the core workflow without changing the product’s underlying structure: activity was organized by context instead of only by date, filters made it easier to move between all activity, games, and practices, and the court view became a supporting expandable panel instead of dominating the screen. Because Pulse could become an adoption path into Helix, I carried the same mental model forward so upgrading would feel like an expansion, not like learning a new product.

Decision 03

Create Favorite Stats as a reusable capability

This was not a before/after redesign. It was a new capability I established from a Helix ask, then translated into Pulse so coaches could keep the most important stats large, visible, and easy to communicate during live game moments.

Key Decisions

The core tension was product fit. Helix supported a deeper, sensor-based analytics membership, while Pulse needed to stand on its own as a more affordable, easier-to-adopt game-day product. I focused on the moments where design could create the most value: clarifying Pulse’s role, aligning the app ecosystem under the ShotTracker brand, creating a consistent navigation model from Helix to Pulse, and introducing Favorite Stats as a configurable capability that could scale across membership experiences.

This was not a blank-slate redesign. Some parts of the broader system had to remain intact, so the work centered on high-impact UX and brand changes that made Pulse feel purposeful for new programs without disconnecting it from the ShotTracker product family or the future Helix upgrade path.

Reflection

Pulse was a useful reminder that product design is often about creating clarity inside constraints. The opportunity was not to redesign the entire ShotTracker ecosystem, but to identify where a lighter product tier could deliver immediate game-day value and still feel connected to the broader platform.

The strongest design work came from balancing adoption and continuity: making Pulse easier for new programs to understand, reducing confusion across product naming and store presence, and preserving familiar concepts that could support a future move into the deeper Helix experience.
