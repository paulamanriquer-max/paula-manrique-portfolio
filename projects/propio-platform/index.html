The Challenge

Propio’s products had grown independently over time. Each product had its own UI patterns, components, navigation, and approach to users and access. Similar experiences were designed and built differently across products, and sometimes within the same product.

As major redesigns began, the work revealed a bigger platform problem: improving individual products would not be enough. Propio needed a shared foundation that could make each product feel connected without forcing every workflow into the same mold.

Scaling the System

The first design system solved the immediate web product need, but I knew it would eventually need to support a broader ecosystem. Tokens were embedded inside the web library, which worked until the system needed to expand into mobile.

As the design team grew, we separated primitive and semantic tokens into a shared foundation, moved assets into their own library, and allowed web and mobile libraries to use the same underlying design language.

Foundation First

Decision 01

Start with the system, not the screens

The design strategy was to solve the foundation first, then let product screens inherit the same patterns. I built the web design system alongside active product work, reducing design and engineering debt while teams continued shipping.

Decision 02

Create one entry point for the ecosystem

Part of the MegaApp strategy was standardizing access. Instead of asking users to remember a different URL for every product, I designed a single login experience with SSO and a shared product App Switcher. After signing in, users could eventually navigate to the different apps they had access to from one consistent place.

The fragmentation went beyond two applications

The need for a shared system became clearer when looking across the wider product ecosystem. Each application had evolved independently, with its own navigation, components, and interaction patterns. To create a connected experience, we needed to move beyond one-off redesigns and establish a system that could scale across the entire platform.

Propio One

A different navigation structure, component library, and information hierarchy created its own distinct product experience.

WFOS

A legacy, data-heavy interface relied on dense workflows and patterns unique to scheduling.

Analytics

An outdated visual language and navigation model introduced yet another set of patterns across the platform.

Proof Through Products

Translation Portal and Interpreter Portal became the clearest proof points for the platform direction. Each product served a different workflow, but the redesigns showed they could share navigation, components, interaction patterns, and visual language without losing what made each product specific.

Before / after

Translation Portal

The legacy Translation Portal relied on dense tables, inconsistent controls, and product-specific navigation. The redesign introduced shared navigation, reusable components, and interaction patterns that simplified complex translation workflows and helped establish the foundation for the unified platform.

Before / after

Interpreter Portal

The legacy Interpreter Portal had its own navigation, visual language, and page structure. The redesign brought it into the shared product system with consistent navigation and components, while preserving the workflows, status, and performance information unique to interpreters.

Beyond Consistency

Making products look consistent was not enough. A unified platform needed a shared way to think about organizations, users, product access, roles, and permissions. Without that model, each product would continue to define users differently.

The Path Forward

Bringing six applications into one ecosystem at once was not realistic. I created a two-phase strategy that let teams keep shipping while moving toward the same platform direction.

Different experiences Different architecture

Unify

Future State

One platform does not mean one giant product

The platform should own shared services like organizations, users, roles, authentication, notifications, search, settings, help, and app switching. Products should keep the specialized workflows and business logic that make them valuable.

Leadership Outcome

I started this transformation as Propio’s first product designer. Today, the platform direction is scaling through the design team. I lead two product designers who are redesigning WFOS and the mobile Interpreter app while extending the web and mobile design libraries.

I continue to supervise the work, helping ensure each experience reuses shared patterns, stays aligned to the broader UX vision, and still makes room for the nuances of each product’s users, workflows, and goals.

Reflection

What started as a design system initiative became a broader transformation of how Propio approaches its product ecosystem. I did not set out to make six products simply look more consistent. I created the foundation for them to eventually work as one platform. That shared foundation reduced duplicated design decisions across product teams and helped accelerate redesign work across web and mobile.
